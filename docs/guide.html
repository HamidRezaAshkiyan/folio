Minimal Field Guide

The Bullet Journal Method, distilled.

Created by Ryder Carroll, the Bullet Journal (BuJo) is a mindfulness practice disguised as a productivity system. No templates, no elaborate spreads — just a pen, a notebook, and short objective bullets that help you track the past, order the present, and design the future.

1. Rapid Logging 2. Task States 3. Signifiers 4. Core Modules 5. Collections 6. Migration & Reflection 7. Carry to Paper
01 · The Language

Rapid Logging

Rapid Logging replaces long-form sentences with short, scannable entries categorized by a single symbol at the start of the line. Every entry has four parts: a Topic (the page header), a Page Number, a Bullet, and a Short Sentence.

.

Task / Action

Something you need to do. Keep it concrete and actionable — "Email Maya the layout proof", not "Zine".

o

Event

Date-related experiences — meetings, birthdays, trips. Logged objectively before or after they happen.

-

Note

Facts, ideas, quotes, or observations you don't want to forget. Zero pressure to act on them.

=

Emotion (Folio Extension)

A brief check-in on how you feel. Tracking mood alongside tasks reveals what gives or drains your energy.

2026-11-25 · WEDNESDAY Notebook p. 42
.Finalize studio lighting sketch before critique
oCoffee with Maya at Blue Bottle — discussed cover stock
-120gsm cream uncoated paper feels warmer under north light
=Calm and focused after walking without headphones
×Order fountain pen ink refill (Iroshizuku Shin-kai)
02 · Lifecycle

Task States

A task dot (.) is designed to be transformed in place as its fate becomes clear. You never erase history — you mark what happened to it.

×

Completed

Turn . into × when finished. Seeing completed crosses builds honest momentum.

>

Migrated

Moved forward to tomorrow's Daily Log, the Monthly Log, or a dedicated Collection.

<

Scheduled

Pushed back into the Future Log for a later month when it's not actionable yet.

~

Irrelevant / Cancelled

Strike through tasks that no longer matter. Dropping unimportant work is a win, not a failure.

03 · Emphasis

Signifiers (Modifiers)

Signifiers sit immediately to the left of a bullet to give extra context at a glance. Keep them minimal — two is usually enough.

*

Priority (*.)

Marks the 1–3 things that truly deserve your attention today. If everything is starred, nothing is.

!

Inspiration (!-)

Highlights great ideas, insights, or quotes you want to spot quickly when flipping back through pages.

04 · Structure

The Four Core Modules

Every Bullet Journal is built from just four interlocking sections. Together they zoom smoothly from the year down to the hour.

1. Index Index.md

The first pages of your notebook act as a living table of contents. Whenever you start a new Monthly Log or Collection, record its title and page number. If a topic continues later in the book, use Page Threading (e.g. p. 14, 27, 41) to link non-contiguous pages.

2. Future Log Future Log.md

A 6-month horizon divided into monthly buckets. Park upcoming trips, deadlines, and scheduled tasks (<) that belong outside the current month so they stay out of today's noise until their month arrives.

3. Monthly Log YYYY-MM-monthly.md

A two-part bird's-eye view of the current month: a Calendar Page (dates 1–31 with brief events/milestones) and a Monthly Task List (key priorities and unfinished tasks migrated from last month).

4. Daily Log Daily Notes/YYYY-MM-DD.md

Your day-to-day workhorse. Don't pre-draw days ahead of time — write today's date and log as much or as little as the day requires. Some days take three lines; others take two pages.

05 · Modularity

Custom Collections (Spaces)

Whenever related entries start piling up — a book list, a studio project, health notes, or meeting takeaways — give them their own dedicated spread. Turn to the next blank page, give it a title, add it to the Index, and log freely.

In Folio, Custom Collections are called Spaces (stored as plain Markdown files under Spaces/, such as Studio & Work, Reading & Study, and Personal).

06 · The Habit

Migration & Reflection

Writing bullets is only half the method. The real magic happens during Migration — pausing regularly to review open tasks and ask a simple question: "Is this still worth my time?"

AM Reflection

Scan yesterday's open tasks. Pick 1–3 priorities (*) for today. Leave the rest behind or cancel them.

PM Reflection

Cross off completed items (×), note a brief emotion (=), and clear your head before sleep.

Monthly Migration

Set up the new Monthly Log, pull items from the Future Log, and intentionally migrate (>) only what still matters.

Why friction is a feature: Having to consciously move a task forces you to confront busywork. If you've migrated the same task three times, either break it down or strike it out (~).
07 · Analog + Digital

Carry to Paper

Paper is unbeatable for thinking, but you won't always have your notebook open on the bus or at your desk. Folio bridges that gap with a lightweight Carry to Paper tag: every new digital entry starts as [paper:no].

At the end of the day, open your physical notebook, transcribe the entries that belong on paper, and flip them to [paper:ok]. You keep the speed of a keyboard without abandoning the calm of ink and paper.
